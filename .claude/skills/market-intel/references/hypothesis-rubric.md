# Hypothesis rubric

A service architecture hypothesis is a falsifiable proposition about an offer that does not yet
exist. It is not an idea, a positioning statement, or a plan.

## Required structure

Every hypothesis states, in this order:

1. **Buyer** — who signs, in a named role at a named kind of organization.
2. **Problem in their words** — using category language the research actually recorded, cited.
3. **Offer shape** — what is delivered, in what unit of sale.
4. **Delivery model** — who does the work, over what arc, requiring what client effort.
5. **Price logic** — the mechanism, not a number. Why this pricing shape follows from the buyer's
   budget line and the value's observability.
6. **Evidence** — the specific dossier fields and absences it rests on, cited.
7. **Load-bearing assumption** — the one belief that, if false, collapses the hypothesis.
8. **Disconfirming test** — the cheapest action that would produce evidence against it.

## Scoring gate

Score each 1 to 5. A hypothesis scoring 2 or below on any dimension does not go to the user; it
is either strengthened or replaced.

| Dimension | Question | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence grounding** | Does it rest on cited findings rather than plausibility? | Support is "this seems underserved" with no dossier reference |
| **Falsifiability** | Is there a concrete result that would kill it? | The test is "see if clients respond" |
| **Structural distinctness** | Is it a different *shape* from the other two? | It is the same offer at a different price or to a slightly different sector |
| **Gap discrimination** | Is the gap unoccupied rather than unviable? | Absence is treated as opportunity without addressing why nobody is there |
| **Deliverability** | Can the firm as it exists actually deliver it? | It presumes a bench, a product, or a credential the firm does not have |
| **Price coherence** | Does the price mechanism follow from the buyer's budget line? | The pricing model is asserted without reference to how this buyer procures |

## The mandatory three

The three hypotheses must differ structurally, not by degree:

- **H1 Occupy** — takes a specific documented gap.
- **H2 Reframe** — same capability, different buyer, budget line, or unit of sale.
- **H3 Contrarian** — deliberately inverts the category consensus the research documented. It
  must be the one the agent would argue against, and the brief must state that argument.

H3 exists to counter convergence. An agent that studies a category and proposes three ideas will
propose three ideas the category would recognize. H3 is the structural correction.

## Second-order effects

Each hypothesis states at least one second-order consequence of pursuing it, drawn from:

- What it commits the firm to that is hard to reverse.
- What it forecloses (which buyers, positions, or partnerships become unavailable).
- What it signals to existing clients and to competitors.
- What capability it must build, and what that capability's maintenance cost is.

## What the agent does not do

It does not rank the three, recommend one, or predict revenue. Ranking imports assumptions about
the firm's risk appetite and time horizon that only the principal holds. The agent presents the
decision surface; the human decides.
