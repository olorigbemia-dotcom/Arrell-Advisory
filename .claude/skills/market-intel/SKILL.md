# Market and Competitor Intelligence Agent

## What this agent is for

Turning a one-line market question into an **evidence-graded decision instrument**: a set of
competitor dossiers where every claim carries a source, a machine-verified comparison, an
explicit account of what the category is *not* saying, and three testable service architecture
hypotheses that a human accepts, rejects, or edits.

It is not a report generator. A report that reads well and cannot be audited is a failure of
this skill, not a partial success.

## Non-negotiable operating rules

1. **Extraction and interpretation are separate acts.** During extraction you record what a
   source says. You do not decide what it means. Interpretation happens only in Stage 6, and
   only over validated dossiers.
2. **Every extracted field carries provenance.** `source_url`, `retrieved_at`, and a verbatim
   `quote` of 200 characters or less. A field without provenance is not a field; set it to
   `null` with `evidence: "not_public"`.
3. **Absent is a finding, never a zero.** "No pricing published" is a data point about the
   category. Never estimate, infer, or triangulate a price and present it as observed. If you
   reason toward a number, it lives in the interpretation layer labelled as a guess, never in
   a dossier.
4. **A competitor's claim is a claim.** Marketing copy records what a firm *asserts*, not what
   is true. Dossier fields are typed `claimed` unless corroborated by an independent source,
   in which case they are `corroborated`.
5. **The evidence audit is a gate, not a report.** If `evidence_audit.py` fails, you do not
   proceed to hypotheses. You fix the dossiers or you narrow the conclusion.
6. **Stage 8 stops.** The agent does not act on its own hypotheses. It presents them and waits.
7. **Public sources only**, per `references/source-policy.md`. No gated content, no paid-report
   redistribution, no circumvention of paywalls or robots directives, no personal data about
   named individuals beyond a public professional role.

## Run structure

Every invocation creates an immutable run directory:

```
research/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>/
  00-scope.md              Stage 0 scope contract
  01-research-plan.md      Stage 1 plan
  02-competitor-set.md     Stage 2 identified set + inclusion rationale
  dossiers/<slug>.json     Stage 4 one file per competitor
  03-comparison.md         Stage 5 generated by script from dossiers
  04-patterns-gaps.md      Stage 6 interpretation
  05-hypotheses.md         Stage 7 three service architecture hypotheses
  06-decision.md           Stage 8 human verdict, written after approval gate
  audit.json               evidence audit result
```

Runs are never edited in place after a decision is recorded. A revision is a new run that
references the prior run's directory. This makes positioning drift visible over time.

## The pipeline

### Stage 0 — Scope contract  (GATE A, cheap)

Convert the request into an explicit scope before spending any research budget. Write
`00-scope.md` with:

- **Market definition** in the buyer's words, not the seller's.
- **Buyer**: who signs, who champions, who blocks.
- **Segment bounds**: organization size, sector, regulatory regime, geography.
- **Time window**: how recent a source must be to count (default: 18 months for positioning,
  12 months for pricing, 6 months for trends).
- **Explicitly out of scope**: the adjacent things you are *not* researching.
- **Decision this feeds**: what the user will do differently depending on the answer.

Then ask the user **one** confirmation question with `AskUserQuestion`, offering your inferred
scope as the recommended option plus one materially different framing of the market. This is
the cheapest possible correction point; a wrong scope wastes the entire run. Do not ask more
than one question here. If the user passes `--auto-scope`, skip the gate and proceed on your
inference, recording it as an assumption.

### Stage 1 — Research plan

Write `01-research-plan.md` from `templates/research-plan.md`. It states the questions to be
answered, the source classes to be used, the extraction schema version, the stopping rule
(how you know research is done), and the known biases of this method. Show it to the user
inline but do not gate on it.

### Stage 2 — Competitor identification

Identify the competitor set. Tier it, because tiers behave differently:

| Tier | Definition | Typical count |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Same buyer, same problem, same budget line | 4-6 |
| **Adjacent** | Same buyer, neighbouring problem (privacy, risk, MLOps, change management) | 3-5 |
| **Substitute** | What the buyer does instead of hiring anyone (in-house hire, tooling, law firm, Big 4 bundle, do nothing) | 3-4 |
| **Aspirational** | Firms setting the category's language regardless of size overlap | 2-3 |

Write `02-competitor-set.md` recording, for each: why it is in, which tier, and the search path
that surfaced it. **Also record who you deliberately excluded and why** — the exclusion list is
where frame bias hides.

Counter the two identification biases explicitly:
- **Search-ranking bias**: the firms that rank are the firms that invest in SEO, not the firms
  that win deals. Deliberately seek at least two competitors via a non-search path (conference
  speaker lists, association member directories, procurement/award notices, analyst mentions).
- **Seed anchoring**: if the user named competitors, treat that list as a hypothesis, not a
  boundary. Add at least one credible name they did not mention.

### Stage 3 — Source collection

Per `references/source-policy.md`. Prefer, in order: the firm's own site (positioning, services,
pricing pages), its public collateral (published rate cards, capability statements, government
schedule listings), third-party corroboration (directories, press, conference programs), then
buyer-side signal (job postings, RFP language, public procurement documents, community
discussion). Record every URL you actually opened, including dead ends.

### Stage 4 — Per-competitor extraction  (fan-out)

For each competitor, dispatch a `competitor-researcher` subagent (`.claude/agents/`). One
competitor per subagent, isolated context. Each returns a dossier JSON conforming to
`references/extraction-schema.md`. Write it to `dossiers/<slug>.json`.

Isolation is deliberate: it prevents one firm's vocabulary from colouring another's extraction,
and it keeps each context small enough that source text does not fall out of the window.

Then run, from the repository root:

```
python3 .claude/skills/market-intel/scripts/validate_dossier.py research/<run>/dossiers/*.json
```

Fix every error before continuing. Warnings are judgement calls; resolve them explicitly.

### Stage 5 — Comparison  (generated, not written)

```
python3 .claude/skills/market-intel/scripts/build_comparison.py research/<run> > research/<run>/03-comparison.md
```

The comparison matrix is produced by code from the dossiers. Do not hand-write it and do not
edit the generated file. If the table is wrong, the dossier is wrong — fix the source of truth.
This is what keeps the comparison honest: the table cannot contain a claim that no dossier
contains, and every cell traces to a footnote.

### Stage 6 — Patterns and gaps

Now, and only now, interpret. Write `04-patterns-gaps.md` with four separated sections:

- **Facts** — what the dossiers establish, each with a citation.
- **Interpretations** — what the facts suggest, each labelled with the facts it rests on.
- **Guesses** — what you suspect but cannot evidence, labelled as such.
- **Absences** — what nobody in the set says, offers, prices, or targets. Weight this section
  hardest. For each absence, state the two competing explanations: *unoccupied* (nobody has
  figured it out) versus *unviable* (everybody tried and it does not sell). An absence you
  cannot distinguish between those is not yet a gap; it is a question.

Also report **category language**: the exact nouns and verbs recurring across the set, and the
terms that appear only in one firm's copy. Vocabulary convergence is a positioning signal.

### Stage 7 — Three service architecture hypotheses

Write `05-hypotheses.md` from `templates/hypothesis-brief.md`, scored against
`references/hypothesis-rubric.md`. The three must be **structurally different**, not three
flavours of one idea. Force separation by construction:

- **H1 — Occupy**: takes a specific gap the evidence supports.
- **H2 — Reframe**: keeps current capability, changes the buyer, budget line, or unit of sale.
- **H3 — Contrarian**: deliberately does the opposite of the category consensus the evidence
  documented. It must be the one you would argue against.

Each hypothesis states: the buyer, the problem in their words, the offer shape, the delivery
model, the price logic, the evidence it rests on, **the assumption that must be true**, and
**the cheapest test that would disconfirm it**.

Then run:

```
python3 .claude/skills/market-intel/scripts/evidence_audit.py research/<run>
```

This checks citation coverage across the interpretation and hypothesis documents. If it fails,
you weaken the claim or strengthen the evidence. You do not proceed on a failed audit.

### Stage 8 — Approval gate  (GATE B, hard stop)

Present the three hypotheses and ask the user to **approve, reject, or edit** each one, using
`AskUserQuestion` with one question per hypothesis (max three questions). Present, for each:
one-line thesis, the assumption it depends on, and the disconfirming test.

Record the verdict verbatim in `06-decision.md`, including the user's edits and reasoning. Then
stop. Do not begin executing an approved hypothesis in the same turn; that is a separate,
explicitly-requested piece of work.

## Stopping rules

Stop researching a competitor when three consecutive sources add no new field. Stop the run
when the direct tier is covered and two of three remaining tiers have at least two entries.
Depth beyond that buys precision you will not use, and every extra source is another chance to
introduce an unverified claim.
