# Service architecture hypotheses — fixture

## H1 — Occupy: size-explicit governance for a named band

**Buyer.** Chief Risk Officer at a regulated organization. **Problem.** Neither firm states an org-size fit, so mid-band buyers self-disqualify. [[example-advisory]]
**Offer.** A size-bounded assessment. **Delivery.** Solo principal over six weeks. **Price.** Fixed fee anchored to the one published figure in the set. https://secondfirm.com/services
**Evidence.** Both dossiers record no org_size field. [[example-advisory]] [[second-firm]]
**Assumption.** Buyers in that band are actually filtering on size rather than sector.
**Test.** Ten qualification calls; count how many raise size unprompted before sector.
**Second-order.** Commits the firm to a segment that forecloses enterprise pursuit for a period.

## H2 — Reframe: same capability, procurement as the buyer

**Buyer.** Procurement lead rather than the risk owner. **Problem.** Assessment-led entry is the shared shape across 2 of 2 firms, so the risk-owner door is crowded. [[example-advisory]] [[second-firm]]
**Offer.** A vendor-governance review. **Delivery.** Fixed engagement. **Price.** Per-vendor unit rather than per-program. **Evidence.** No firm in the set prices per unit. [[second-firm]]
**Assumption.** Vendor governance holds a separate budget line from the AI program budget.
**Test.** Read five public RFPs and check whether vendor governance is scoped separately.
**Second-order.** Signals a compliance-adjacent position that may weaken the strategic frame.

## H3 — Contrarian: publish pricing against category consensus

**Buyer.** Same risk owner. **Problem.** 1 of 2 firms publishes nothing, and pricing silence is the category default. [[example-advisory]]
**Offer.** A fully published rate card. **Delivery.** Unchanged. **Price.** Transparent and fixed. **Evidence.** Only one figure is recoverable across the whole set. https://secondfirm.com/services
**Assumption.** Opacity is inertia rather than a deliberate qualification mechanism.
**Test.** Publish one price for one offer for sixty days and measure inbound qualification rate.
**The argument against this.** Opacity may be load-bearing; a published price anchors negotiation downward and cannot be withdrawn quietly.
**Second-order.** Irreversible in perception terms; competitors gain a reference point.
