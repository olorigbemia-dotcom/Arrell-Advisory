## Known biases of this method

- **Search-ranking bias.** Firms that rank invest in SEO; ranking is not market share. Mitigated,
  not solved, by the non-search paths above.
- **Public-copy bias.** Everything here is what firms choose to publish. Firms winning work
  through relationships may be nearly invisible to this method, and their absence will look
  like a gap.
- **Small n.** This set describes the category's public language. It does not describe the
  market. Claims about realized demand require records-class sources.
- **Snapshot bias.** A single retrieval date reads as "how things are". It is how things were
  on one day. Trend claims need dated sources.
- **Convergence pressure.** Studying a category makes its consensus feel like truth. H3 exists
  to counter this structurally.
